# AI Literacy: Learn, Create, and Work More Confidently

**CHI+AI conversation:** Heather Masters and Ralph Babusci
**Audience:** Chicagoans and the general public
**Length:** 40 minutes, plus optional 5-minute Q&A
**Tool:** Microsoft Copilot on the web (`copilot.microsoft.com`), signed in with a personal Microsoft account

## The simple message

AI can help us learn, create, and get work started. It can also make mistakes. We stay in charge:

1. **Ask clearly.**
2. **Check the answer.**
3. **Choose what to do.**

## Before the talk

- Open Copilot and sign in with a personal Microsoft account. Do not use a work or school account for this demonstration.
- Before screen sharing, make sure your account details and previous chats are not visible.
- Use only the made-up examples in this talk.
- Invite participants to follow along at `copilot.microsoft.com` with their own personal account if they choose. Watching is fine; no one has to sign in.
- Do not ask participants to share personal information, private chats, or files.
- If sign-in or a feature is unavailable, continue with the prepared example. Features and settings can change.
- Invite people to watch, think, or pass. No one has to use AI.

## 0:00-3:00 — Welcome: start with the audience

**Say:**

> Welcome. Today we'll try a few simple ways Microsoft Copilot can help us learn, create, and get work started. You don't need to be an AI expert—and you don't have to use AI to take part.

**Ask:**

> Where have you noticed AI in everyday life—recommendations, maps, captions, or chat?

Take two or three answers.

**Ralph and Heather:** Briefly share one everyday example each. Keep it conversational.

**Say:**

> Our three habits today are simple: ask clearly, check the answer, and choose what to do.

## 3:00-7:00 — What AI can and cannot do

**Say:**

> Copilot can write a first draft, explain an idea, or help us explore options. It predicts a response from patterns and information. That response can sound sure and still be wrong.

> Think of it as a helpful starting point—not an expert, and not the final decision-maker.

**Ask:**

> What would you want to check before relying on an answer about health, money, safety, or another person?

**Key line:**

> A confident answer is not the same as a correct answer.

## 7:00-13:00 — Personal Copilot: terms, data, and choices

**Say:**

> Please open `copilot.microsoft.com`. If you want to follow along, sign in with a personal Microsoft account—not a work or school account. You can also just watch.

> Before using a service, it is worth knowing its basic rules. The personal Copilot terms say Microsoft does not own your prompts and replies, but you give Microsoft permission to process them to run and improve Copilot. The terms also say some processing may be done by people as well as computers.

> That means a personal Copilot chat is not a private diary. Don't enter passwords, private messages, sensitive personal details, or work or school information.

### The simple answer about model training

**Say:**

> There is a difference between using a chat to run and improve a service and using it to train the main AI model. Microsoft's current Copilot privacy information says prompts, replies, and file contents in the Copilot app are not used to train its foundation models. Microsoft still processes information to provide, protect, and improve the service, as described in its terms and privacy statement.

> So: “not used to train the main model” does not mean “nothing is saved or used.”

### What people can control

**Say:**

> When you are signed in, Copilot keeps chat history so you can return to it. You can export or delete Copilot activity from your Microsoft Privacy Dashboard. In Copilot settings, you can review choices such as saved memories, connected experiences, web search, and personalized ads. The exact settings may change.

Show, but do not expose account details:

1. Open the profile or settings menu.
2. Find **Personalization** and review the available choices.
3. Point to the Microsoft Privacy Dashboard for exporting or deleting activity history.

**Ask:**

> What is one thing you would keep out of a personal AI chat?

**Key line:**

> Use a personal account for personal experiments. Keep private and work information out of public Copilot.

Tell the audience that Microsoft's terms and privacy pages are linked below. These are plain-language notes, not legal advice; the official pages are the source of truth.

## 13:00-18:00 — Demo 1: use Copilot to learn

Open Copilot and enter:

> Explain generative AI in plain language in three short sentences. Give one everyday example. Include links to sources I can check, and tell me what you are unsure about.

**As you review the response, say:**

> This gives us a place to start. Let's open a source and see whether it supports the answer.

**Ask:**

- Is the explanation easy to understand?
- Which claim would you check?
- Does the source actually support that claim?

If a link is missing or unhelpful, model a simple follow-up:

> Please give me a reliable source for that point. If you can't find one, say so.

**Takeaway:**

> Copilot can help us learn. We still check important facts.

## 18:00-28:00 — Demo 2: use Copilot to create

Introduce the made-up event:

- **Repair & Share**
- Saturday, October 17
- 10:00 a.m. to noon
- Eastside Community Room
- Neighbors may bring a small household item to discuss repair options
- For community members and families
- Registration details are not decided

Start with a vague prompt:

> Write an invitation for a community repair event.

**Ask the audience:**

> What might Copilot have to guess?

Then give it the facts and boundaries:

> Write a short, friendly invitation for the fictional Repair & Share event. Use only these facts: Saturday, October 17, 10 a.m. to noon, Eastside Community Room. Community members and families may bring a small household item to discuss repair options. Do not invent costs, repair services, or registration details. List anything the organizer still needs to decide.

Read the result together. Ask the audience to spot a supported fact and anything that needs checking.

**Say:**

> Copilot helped us make a draft. We supplied the facts and decide what to keep.

## 28:00-32:00 — Check before you share

Show or read one deliberately incorrect detail in the invitation—for example, the wrong time or an invented registration link.

**Ask:**

> What should we check first? Where is the original information?

Model three easy checks:

1. Compare names, dates, times, and numbers with the original facts.
2. Open links and check that they say what Copilot claims.
3. If something matters, check another reliable source or ask a qualified person.

For images or videos, add:

> Don't decide only by how real it looks. Ask who shared it and whether a trusted source confirms it.

## 32:00-36:00 — Demo 3: use Copilot to get work started

Use only this made-up example:

> I helped at a community book swap. I sorted books and answered visitor questions.

Ask Copilot:

> Make this sentence clearer for a resume. Keep it truthful. Do not add numbers, results, tools, or responsibilities. Tell me if you need more information.

Compare the original with the rewrite. Point out any added claim.

**Say:**

> Better wording is useful. Made-up experience is not. Only include claims you can stand behind.

**Ask:**

> Does the new version still sound true to the person who did the work?

## 36:00-38:00 — Keep people in charge

**Ask Ralph and the audience:**

- Who could benefit from using AI here?
- Who might be left out or affected?
- Is there a good reason not to use AI for this task?

**Say:**

> AI can offer ideas. People bring local knowledge, care, and responsibility. We can choose to use it, change the answer, or walk away.

## 38:00-40:00 — Close: three questions

Before using or sharing an AI answer, pause:

1. **Is it safe to share this information?**
2. **Have I checked what matters?**
3. **Do I want to use this answer—and will I stand behind it?**

Invite everyone to try one small, low-risk task only if they want to: ask for an explanation, brainstorm ideas, or make a public paragraph easier to read. Check the answer before using it.

**Final words:**

> Copilot can help us get started. We ask clearly, check the answer, and choose what to do.

## Optional 5-minute Q&A

If the room is quiet, ask:

- What would you like to try Copilot for?
- What would you never enter into a public AI tool?
- When should a person—not AI—make the decision?

## One-minute audience takeaway

**Ask clearly. Check the answer. Choose what to do.**
Use made-up or public information. Check important claims. You are always free to say no.
